## Virginia opossum V1R00 — the faint sells on paws-in-the-air, not belly shape

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1070; `work/animals/draw_virginia_opossum.py`,
  `art/animals/virginia-opossum/virginia-opossum-V1R00.{vectorcraft,svg,@2x.png}`
- Applies to: animals field plates; recumbent/prone poses
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/virginia-opossum-V1R00@2x.png`. Three passes on the
"playing dead" read:

1. A recumbent body drawn as a convex-bottomed oval floats: no matter
   how big the contact shadow, a curve touching the ground at one point
   reads as a ball, not a body. The belly bottom needs a long, nearly
   straight run at a single y, dipping just past the floor crest, plus
   grass tufts overlapping the contact line.
2. What actually sells the faint is the feet: paws curled in the air on
   thin wrists rising off the TOP edge of the body. Two forepaws read;
   a third paw near the rump makes it unmistakable — the classic
   four-feet-up pose needs no perfect belly geometry.
3. A recumbent subject's spine is nearly straight — a dome crest makes
   a balloon, a low flat arc makes a loaf.

Also: a "belly light" ellipse inside a grey body reads as a pale window —
keep interior washes under ~16 opacity and off-centre.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` known-traps: *prone/recumbent
subjects need a flat bottom contact line — a convex belly reads as a
floating ball. The pose carries on the extremities: feet in the air,
not the belly's curvature.*
