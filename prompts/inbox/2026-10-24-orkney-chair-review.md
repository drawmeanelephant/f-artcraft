## Orkney chair V1R00 — wrap must be walls, not flippers

- Kind: analysis
- Source: issue #1116; driver `work/furniture/orkney-chair-V1R00.py`;
  native and proofs `art/furniture/orkney-chair/` +
  `work/out/orkney-chair-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: furniture field plates, three-quarter objects
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Documented form (V&A Eunson type): low timber chair, tall coiled-straw back
curving around the sitter; no hood, drawer or tartan. Fifteen coil courses
computed along the panel's side curves, dashed binding columns, a side
return for thickness.

Two failed cues and their fixes: (1) the back's bottom edge ended just above
the seat top — a bright gap made the straw panel float; dropping the bottom
edge behind the seat slab joined them. (2) The wrap was first drawn as two
small detached "ears" that read as flippers/armrests; extending them into
low side walls that hug the seat corners, each carrying its own coil
strokes, made the back visibly curve around the sitter's space.

### Proposed change

For furniture plates: always overlap a back panel's bottom edge under the
seat — never let a gap show at the joint — and when a part must read as
"wrapping", continue its surface texture onto the wrapped part or it reads
as a separate appendage.
