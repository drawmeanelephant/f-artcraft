# Star-nosed mole V1R00 — the rosette carries the species, size it up

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1073, `work/animals/draw_star_nosed_mole.py`,
  `art/animals/star-nosed-mole/star-nosed-mole-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: field plates, animals
- Status: pending

### Evidence

First pass drew the 22-ray star at thumbnail scale (ray length ~50, base
r13, w0 5.2) on a fur dome: it read as a sea-urchin belly button, not a
snout. Scaling the rosette ~1.4x (base r17, w0 6.8, top rays 70) and
sinking it into a muzzle-shadow socket made the plate — the star is the
species code, everything else is a dark dome. Two more lessons: the
dark-rose aureole under the rays read as a bruise until it was swapped
for a lighter pink glow at ~26% peak; and palmate paws fail when the
claw fan points inward — mole forepaws rotate permanently outward, so
digit grooves must spread from the wrist line (a single convergent
pivot draws fangs, a spread origin draws fingers).

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` known traps: on feature-led
portraits (star-nosed mole, aye-aye finger, elephant trunk) the feature
should be drawn ~1.3-1.5x larger than anatomical scale before anything
else is tuned — at plate scale the organ is the silhouette.
