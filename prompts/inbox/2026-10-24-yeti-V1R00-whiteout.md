## Yeti V1R00 — whiteout is drawn with veils in front, not absence

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1059; `work/animals/draw_yeti.py`, `art/animals/yeti/yeti-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; pale-on-pale subjects; weather scenes
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/yeti-V1R00@2x.png`. "Owner never home" resolved as: a
figure fully drawn but at plate-contrast minimum — pale fur on a pale
plate, separated only by a 4–5pt slate outline (#5E7684, no warm ink), and
then *re-veiled*: three broad white ellipses at op 14–20 laid OVER the
finished figure, shoulders, legs and head, plus a 14% white "echo" mass
beside it. The whiteout reads because it is a layer, not a desaturation.
Story props: an empty den (snow-capped rock + dark mouth, the plate's only
deep value) and a four-print track line whose soles darken toward the
feet. The print "lip" — a 3pt white arc on the pushed-snow side — is what
keeps the ovals reading as pressed tracks rather than scattered stones.

Working notes: on cold plates swap warm INK for a cold slate everywhere —
one warm outline would have made the whole scene read as dusk instead of
weather. The head/torso junction needed three pale "shoulder shag" strokes
across the seam or the dome read as a mask on a body.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md`: pale-subject plates can go lower
contrast than the snowy-owl lesson suggests IF the outline switches from
ink to a lighter cold slate (≥4pt) and at least one weather veil passes in
front of the figure. Footprints: toe row + raised bright lip on the push
side, darkening toward the walker.
