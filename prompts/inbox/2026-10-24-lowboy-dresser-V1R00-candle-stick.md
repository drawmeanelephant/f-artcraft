## Lowboy dresser V1R00 — a lit prop sells the period better than more joinery

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1195, `work/furniture/draw_lowboy_dresser.py`,
  `art/furniture/lowboy-dresser/lowboy-dresser-V1R00.{vectorcraft,svg,@2x.png}`
- Applies to: field plates — furniture
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Drawn in the highboy's screen-space idiom (flat front + `DX/DY` recede, same
cabriole `leg()`, `batwing()`, `fan()`), which makes the family resemblance
honest — the brief literally calls the lowboy the highboy's shorter cousin.
The plate's mood was carried by one prop: a brass candlestick with a burning
taper on the top, done as drip pan → turned stem → ivory candle → wick →
teardrop flame with a radial glow. It anchors "colonial" at a glance and
doubles as the light source. Joinery nit: a tall drawer bank over the apron
left a large blank rail — a single cockbead moulding strip (shadow line +
raised bead + light line) under the last drawer finished the case for ~3
objects.

### Proposed change

Furniture spec candidates: "One lit prop (candle, lamp, ember) can set a
period plate's whole mood — it is both a light source and an era code."
and "A drawer bank should end in a cockbead or moulding strip, not a bare
rail." Pending until consolidated.
