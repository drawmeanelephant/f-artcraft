## Komodo dragon V1R00 — mood carried by props, menace by proportion

- Kind: analysis
- Source: issue #1042; `work/animals/draw_komodo_dragon.py`, `art/animals/komodo-dragon/komodo-dragon-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; reptiles; scene mood
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/komodo-dragon-V1R00@2x.png`. The dragon reads at a glance:
long low hull, belly fold nearly dragging, bowed legs with pale claws, and
the forked yellow tongue — small feature, outsized read, the two-second
species tell. The "carrion-wind" mood is carried by props rather than
expression: a stripped ribcage the tail curls past, a wilting fern, wet
sheen pools on the leaf litter, mist bands dissolving the trunks. Mist at
opacity 14–18 with strokeWidth 7 stays legible at plate scale where flat
fog ellipses would band.

Working notes: pebbly hide is cheap and effective — ~15 low-opacity fleck
ellipses plus five pale spine bosses sell rough skin better than any
outline texture. Neck folds (three short arcs) keep the head-to-body join
from reading as a tube junction. The tail needs to be fatter than feels
right at the hip — a thin tail reads "generic lizard," a thick tapering
one reads "komodo."

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md`: reptile plates want the forked
tongue — it is the fastest reptile tell available and costs two strokes.
For moody scenes, story props (bones, shed skin, nests) beat weather
effects; one prop placed where the subject's tail or gaze points does the
work of a paragraph.
