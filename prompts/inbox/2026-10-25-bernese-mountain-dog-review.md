### Evidence

Standing square on an alpine-dusk disc. The tricolor recipe that worked:
jet-black warm-ramp body; markings as *overlay shapes* in order — white
blaze (narrow stripe, forehead to nose front), white chest cross (big
triangle throat to between the forelegs), white socks; rust points as
small ovals at brow, cheek edge, and mid-leg above the socks. Order
matters: rust sits between black and white on the legs, matching the
real marking map.

Calm weight comes from the barrel: level topline, chest deeper than the
belly line, legs as planted columns — no trot diagonal. The drop ear as
a soft hanging triangle beside the skull reads immediately. Tail carried
low with a white tip.

Weak spots: the muzzle still hooks slightly at the nose even after
narrowing the blaze — the lower-lip curve wants flattening; and the
neck ruff + deep chest make a double curve at the throat that reads as
a dewlap (breed-plausible, but borderline).

Survives its critique: unmistakable tricolor, square draft stance, rim
clean, milestone clears by 69pt.

### Proposed change

Tricolor dogs: draw the marking map in layer order (body → bib → blaze →
rust points → socks), each as overlay shapes, and keep the blaze a stripe —
it hooks the silhouette if it wraps the muzzle tip. Candidate for the
consolidated grammar.
