- Applies to: field plates, aposematic / "display" subjects
- Status: pending

### Evidence

The "strobing" brief wanted pulsing warning colour. What sold it at
proof was treating the flash colours as opaque pigment bands drawn
over a dark maroon mantle — acid yellow, pink, violet at ~88% opacity
with warm-ink weight — and reserving translucency for the fins and
sheen. Earlier sea plates taught that glow effects under ~30% read as
fog; display animals need the opposite: bands that refuse to blend.
Papillae got their own bump shapes with pale tips rather than stroke
dashes, so the skin texture survives at @2x.

Walking arms: splayed tapered struts planted on the sand line with a
single strobe band each — the band is what separates "arm" from
"stick". Two raised scout arms ahead carry the walk direction.

### Proposed change

Add to `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` animal notes: "Aposematic
display (cuttlefish, nudibranchs, dart frogs): draw the flash colours
as opaque pigment bands over a dark ground. Translucent or additive
'glow' treatments fog out against the dark plate; the warning has to
be painted, not lit."
