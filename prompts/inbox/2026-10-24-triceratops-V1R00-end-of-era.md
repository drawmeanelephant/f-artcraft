## Triceratops V1R00 — the head must land on the shoulders, not beside them

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1056; `work/animals/draw_triceratops.py`, `art/animals/triceratops/triceratops-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; big-headed profile subjects
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/triceratops-V1R00@2x.png`. First pass placed the skull left
of the frill with only a small wedge for a neck: on the proof the head read
as a second object floating beside the torso, the worst failure a profile
plate can have. Fix that survived critique: a thick neck mass drawn after
the torso that bulges below the frill's lower edge and underlaps the head's
back (drawn later still), so three shapes overlap into one column —
head → neck → chest. A grazing, downcast head reads correctly *because*
the neck visibly carries the weight.

Working notes: epoccipitals are cheap — seven small ink-stroked ellipses
centered on the frill's edge path, half in half out, sell the scalloped
shield better than a scalloped outline. The end-of-era mood is carried by
one bolide trail + a dozen ash specks at opacity ~35; a faint ember
ellipse on the horizon (op. 16) is enough, more would fight the floor.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md`: in profile plates the head may
hang low (grazing poses read well) but the neck must be a drawn mass, not
implied — bridge head back to chest with an overlapping shape or the head
floats. Ceratopsian frill: outline + epoccipital bumps on the edge beats a
scalloped path.
