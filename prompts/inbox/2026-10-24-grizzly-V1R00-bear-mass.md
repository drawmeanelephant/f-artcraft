## Grizzly bear V1R00 — bear mass and the far-bank rule

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1035; `work/animals/draw_grizzly_bear.py`, `art/animals/grizzly-bear/grizzly-bear-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; big quadrupeds in water
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/grizzly-bear-V1R00@2x.png`. Two findings:

1. A bear is its belly line. First pass put the belly at y≈646 with ~70px
   legs and a long smooth dome from nose to hump — it rendered as a giant
   capybara/molar, not a grizzly. Fix: drop the belly ~25px so the legs are
   short columns, lengthen the muzzle another ~20px left and lower the
   head, and break the head→hump run with dark ruff strokes at the neck.
   The hump itself needs to be a broad landslide crest, not a bump.
2. Trees standing IN water read as mangrove. The pale alder trunks ended
   at the waterline among the boulders and the scene read wrong until a
   far-bank band (a wavy dark scrub strip + two conifer spires) went in
   across the water's top edge — the trunks then grow from land and the
   band fills the mid-distance void. Rule: any upright plant behind a
   waterline needs a bank to stand on.

Also: a leaping salmon reads best head-UP pointing upstream — the same
direction as the waiting bear's gaze. Its tail fin is a two-notch zigzag,
not a fan.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` known-traps: heavy quadrupeds
(bears, boar, hippos) need the belly LOW — legs shorter than the torso is
deep — plus a ruff break between head and hump or the whole animal reads
as a loaf. Water scenes need a far-bank strip if anything grows at the
shoreline.
