## Bob Dylan V1R00 — a harp rack is two wire strokes + one reeded bar

- Kind: analysis
- Source: issue #1540; `work/musicians/draw_bob_dylan.py`,
  `art/musicians/bob-dylan/bob-dylan-V1R00.{vectorcraft,svg}`,
  proof `work/out/bob-dylan-V1R00@2x.png`
- Applies to: musicians milestone series; face-level props
- Status: pending

### Evidence

One render pass held up; check_rim clean.

- **The harmonica rack is a three-part glyph:** two thin wire strokes
  (3.5pt, cool grey) running from the collar up the jaw sides, then a
  small metal bar across the mouth with 5–7 reed ticks. No cage, no
  springs — the wire sides alone imply the frame. Anything more would
  fight the face.
- **The harp covers the mouth entirely** — put a lower-lip hint beneath
  the bar so the face still resolves. The mouth itself is behind the
  instrument, same rule as the Armstrong mouthpiece but stronger: the
  prop IS the mouth zone.
- **Tousled curls = lobed cloud + ringlet hooks.** The ringlets are
  half-arc hooks (`a{r},{r} 0 1 1 …`) spaced around the perimeter —
  reads "unkempt" where Hendrix's read "explosion". Careful with top
  reach: this pass ran tall and reads slightly beehive; widen the sides
  before raising the crown.
- Small-body acoustics stay proportionate — body ~140 wide (vs the
  Elvis dreadnought ~160) reads as the 00/J-45 class.
- Working-angle neck (~27°) clears the rack bar and keeps the fret hand
  below chin height — the harp stays the hero.

### Proposed change

For the musicians note: face-level prop rule — the prop may cover the
mouth but must leave one small feature (lower lip, chin edge) so the
face doesn't read as masked; rack/holder props are frame-implying
wires, not drawn frames.
