## Thunderbird V1R00 — storm bank and tail-fan lessons

- Kind: analysis
- Source: issue #1145, `work/animals/draw_thunderbird.py`,
  `art/animals/thunderbird/thunderbird-V1R00.{vectorcraft,svg}`
- Applies to: field plates (animals), sky-weather scenes
- Status: pending

### Evidence

First pass drew the cloud underbelly as one banded path closing on the top
rim arc; the arc-close left two amber flaps diagonal across the rim corners —
an A-close band is the wrong tool for a *painted* under-edge. Swapped to
three radial-gradient washes tucked under the billow line; reads as lit
cloud-belly with no rim artifacts. Tail: feather shapes drawn as thin
`L l l` zigzags collapsed into dangling strings; broad wedge paths with
rounded tip quads read as a real fan. Storm grammar that worked: dark bank
closing on the top arc, amber horizon band behind the subject, bolt kept
thin (5pt) and ~75 opacity so it stays weather, not cartoon.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` known traps: sky decor that meets
the disc edge should close on the arc (floor chords already do this for
ground) — but lit/atmospheric bands should stay as interior washes; only
opaque silhouettes belong on the rim.
