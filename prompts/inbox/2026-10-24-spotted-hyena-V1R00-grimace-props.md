## Spotted hyena V1R00 — a grimace is pulled wide, and props carry mood

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1096; `work/animals/draw_spotted_hyena.py`,
  `art/animals/spotted-hyena/spotted-hyena-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; mouths with intent and story props
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/spotted-hyena-V1R00@2x.png`. The "laugh that is not a
laugh" needed the opposite of the jaguar's downturned sulk: the lip line
is pulled wide and level with the corners drawn hard back, a dark mouth
wedge behind, and small ivory carnassial tips dropping over the lower
edge. Level-plus-wide reads as a grimace where a curved line would have
read as a smile.

The "bone-crack" mood was carried by a prop, not the face: a marrow bone
snapped at the middle (two halves whose jagged ends mirror each other,
dark marrow strokes inside) pinned between the paws, plus flying splinters
above the break and old bone chips on the dust line. One recognisable prop
at plate scale did more for the brief than more face detail would have.

Night-plate note: the moon halo at 14% opacity is what sells "night" more
than the dark disc; acacia silhouettes at 75% on the horizon read clean
because their whole canopy stays inside the disc.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md`: when a brief names a behaviour or
mood ("bone-crack", "alarm", "courtship"), consider a single explicit prop
under the subject before adding face detail — the snapped bone pinned by
paws did the work here. Mouth direction table for carnivore portraits:
upturned = plush toy, level+downturned corners = sulk (jaguar),
level+wide with dark interior and tooth tips = grimace (this plate).
