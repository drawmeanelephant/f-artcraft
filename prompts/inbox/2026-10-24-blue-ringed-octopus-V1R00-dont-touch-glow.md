## Blue-ringed octopus V1R00 — fanned arms need a web, not a gap

- Kind: lesson
- Source: issue #1085; `work/animals/draw_blue_ringed_octopus.py`, `art/animals/blue-ringed-octopus/blue-ringed-octopus-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: animals field plates; multi-limbed subjects
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/blue-ringed-octopus-V1R00@2x.png`. First pass rooted eight
tapered-ribbon arms under the head with bare plate showing between the
roots: on the proof the animal read as a spider — long jointed legs under a
round body. Fix that survived critique: a shallow scalloped web-skirt
crescent drawn across the arm bases after the arms, plus short hooked tip
curls as stroked caps past each ribbon end. With the gap filled the splayed
roots became draped arms and the piece reads octopus, not arachnid.
Ribbon tips also end blunt — a 2-segment curl stroke beyond the last
tangent is cheap and lands the tips on the rock.

The warning mood works: electric-blue ring strokes (w6) with a soft
per-ring glow ellipse (op. 22) and one big cool aura (op. 10) behind the
whole animal read as aposematic flash at plate scale without lighting the
backdrop. Small rings (16x12) on the arms survive @2x; anything thinner
would not.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md`: when a subject's limbs fan from a
shared root zone, bridge the roots with a filled skirt/crescent — bare
background between limb origins reads as legs, not limbs.
