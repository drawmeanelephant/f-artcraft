## david-bowie V1R00 — build asymmetry into the silhouette, not a floating wing

- Kind: lesson / analysis
- Source: issue #1542; `work/musicians/draw_david_bowie.py`,
  `art/musicians/david-bowie/david-bowie-V1R00.{vectorcraft,svg}`,
  proof `work/out/david-bowie-V1R00@2x.png`
- Applies to: milestone cards / costume asymmetry, mullet-era hair
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Three render-and-critique loops:

1. A collar drawn as a separate closed shape beside the neck read as a
   floating oven-mitt — it never visually joined the suit. Fix: the
   asymmetric peaked collar went INTO the suit's own top edge (one
   smooth silhouette rising to a point beside the jaw). Same lesson as
   the Beethoven locks: costume features belong to the garment's
   outline, not to spare shapes.
2. The mullet's back length first drawn as two face-height falls read
   as curtain sidelocks (the Joni curtains, wrong cut). Mullet anatomy:
   spiky crown via out/in point pairs, sides cropped AT ear level, the
   back length drawn BEFORE the head as narrow slivers under the jaw.
3. "Cheekbones doing the likeness" worked as a two-stroke pair: a light
   blade stroke above a soft hollow-shade wedge, both angled down toward
   the nose. Hollows alone read as bruises; blades alone read as scars.

`check_rim` clean; `ZIGGY STARDUST · 1972` 405pt wide, clears ~65pt.

### Proposed change

For asymmetric costume: cut the asymmetry into the garment silhouette
(diagonal neckline, peaked collar point in the path itself). For
mullets: crown spikes in the outline, cropped sides, nape slivers
behind the head — never face-height side falls. Destination:
milestone-cards note at next consolidation.
