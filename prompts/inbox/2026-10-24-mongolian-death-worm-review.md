### Evidence

A worm has no face, so the read is all silhouette + skin. What worked: a
two-segment cubic spine (rise, then a restrained head hook) drawn as a
tapered ribbon (w 44→30) — the first pass used a hard crook and w34→24 and
read as a candy cane; bluntness is the species code, so keep the tip wide
and the hook shallow. Segment rings are curved chords across the ribbon at
spine samples, bowed slightly tail-ward — regular even spacing reads worm,
not snake. A moist sheen stroke along the lit side + a darker belly stroke
do the wet-skin work.

Folklore restraint: two 3pt zigzag strokes at ~40% opacity near the head
read as static crackle; at higher opacity they read as antennae, which a
worm must not have. Sand clinging to the lower third (a few dark specks)
sells "just surfaced" better than more hole detail. Watch ground strokes
near the rim: the dune ridges at y>770 exceeded r430 and tripped
`check_rim.py` — clamp decoration endpoints inside the chord, the same trap
as the arc-close rule.
