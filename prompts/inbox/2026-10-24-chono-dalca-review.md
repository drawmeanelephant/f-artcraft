## Chono dalca V1R00 — seam reads once the boat sits in the water

- Kind: analysis
- Source: issue #1114; driver `work/vehicles/chono-dalca-V1R00.py`;
  native and proofs `art/vehicles/chono-dalca/` + `work/out/chono-dalca-V1R00.*`
- Applies to: vehicles / watercraft field plates
- Status: pending

### Evidence

Drawn per the Museo Chileno de Arte Precolombino account: two side planks
bent with fire, sewn with culeu-bark cordage to a longer central plank;
pointed rising ends; no sail or engine detail (brief forbids), one plain
paddle with its blade in the water.

First pass floated the hull above the water chord — with the water segment
starting below the keel, the pale headland band showed through the gap and
the canoe read as levitating. Raising the chord to y=566 so the keel dips
below the waterline fixed it; the ripples then sit where the hull actually
pierces the surface. Second pass also rotated the seam stitches ~35° off
perpendicular: square ticks read as rivets, diagonal ones read as lashing.
Converging stitches at the tips sell the three-plank joint.

### Proposed change

For watercraft plates: set the water chord a few points above the hull's
lowest curve so the subject is visibly immersed, and draw lashings/stitches
on the diagonal, not square to the seam. Candidate for `prompts/series/`
field-plates traps list at next consolidation.
