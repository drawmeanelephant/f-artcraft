## Black-tailed prairie dog V1R00 — the upright column wants a lean and a lap

### Evidence

Proof `work/out/black-tailed-prairie-dog-V1R00@2x.png`. Findings:

1. The sentinel column sells on two cues, not posture physics: forepaws
   folded ON the chest (two small dark mitts with digit ticks) and a
   slightly convex chest line against a straighter back. A symmetrical
   column reads as a bowling pin; the chest/belly asymmetry reads as an
   animal watching.
2. The species' name lives in the tail: the black tip must be a
   distinct patch hugging the stub's end, and the tail must clear the
   mound silhouette to be seen — it kicked out behind at ~30° reads
   instantly.
3. "A town" needed only two dark upright blobs + mound ellipses on the
   crest at ~85-95 opacity; more detail would fight the subject. Colony
   reads at silhouette scale only.
4. A pale "muzzle light" wedge at 55 opacity reads as a scar/slash;
   the same shape at ~38 reads as pale cheek fur. Interior washes on a
   mid-tone coat stay under ~45.

### Proposed change

For `prompts/series/20-field-plates.md` known-traps: *upright subjects
are carried by chest-vs-back asymmetry and folded forepaws, not by
column symmetry. Background herd/colony reads want silhouette blobs at
≤10% of subject scale — never drawn with interior detail.*
